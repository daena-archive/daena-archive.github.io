Start with one place, one person, and one event. Ten unhurried minutes with three connected records teaches more about Daena than an afternoon of empty setup.

The names below are examples. Use your own world, or follow along with Eldermere as a placeholder.

## The exercise

1. **Create a place in Lore, such as Eldermere.** Open Lore, choose New, and give the record a name. Write a few sentences in its document — enough to remember what the place feels like. Leave structured fields alone for now.
2. **Create a person and relate them to the place.** Create a second Lore entry, for example Mira Vale, then link the person to Eldermere. The link works in both directions: the place shows the person, and the person shows the place.
3. **Add an encounter in Timeline and select both records as context.** Create an encounter, give it a date, and add the person and the place as participants and location. (Encounters carry participants and locations; plain events carry dates and era links.) Keep the date simple — history should add context, not bookkeeping.
4. **Open Writing Studio and reference the person from a short scene.** Create a manuscript, write a paragraph, and link the person's name to their record. The manuscript keeps readable text; Daena keeps the underlying link to the stable record.

## What to notice

Different modules contribute views and meaning, while the records remain part of one archive. The person on the map, in the event, and in the manuscript is still one entity — renaming them later does not create copies.

If something looks thin at this stage, that is expected. A record can remain a few sentences now and grow into a full reference entry later without being moved into another system.
