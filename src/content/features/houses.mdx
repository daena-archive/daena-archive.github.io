## Families that stay with the archive

A person lives once in the archive. Houses does not make a second copy for a family view. It links the same people you already write about — with their articles, dates, and portraits in Lore — into parents and children, partners, and members of a house. The links work in both directions, so moving from a house to a person and back keeps context close.

## Houses that hold lineage together

A house is a durable lineage, dynasty, or household — a way to name the group that binds people across generations. A person can belong to more than one house, and each belonging can carry its own role. The collection stays calm and searchable, like the rest of the archive, and turning Houses off hides the views without deleting people or the relationships between them.

## A tree around where you are

The Tree shows a readable neighborhood around a selected person or house — close ancestors and descendants, siblings, co-parents, and partners — rather than drawing the entire project at once. Expand one branch at a time, keep what matters in view, and open any person back in Lore when you want more detail. History stays in Timeline; this view is for how people belong together.
